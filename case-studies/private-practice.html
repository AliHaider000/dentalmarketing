Before this started

Zero is not the same as unknown.

The practice's own team could say honestly that the website had never produced a traceable lead. That was not because it produced none. It was because nobody had ever installed anything to find out.

01

The phone rings and nothing records it

Almost every new patient calls the office before they book. No call tracking existed, so every one of those calls was invisible to every report anyone had ever been shown. A practice can be busy and still have no idea which half of the marketing is working.

02

A site built to be looked at, not found

No analytics installed. Broken links. A speed score of 53 out of 100. 94% of images oversized and 100% of them missing alt text. Not one page written around what a patient in that town actually types into Google.

03

Ten years behind on authority

Four practices within a few miles had been investing in search for a decade. The nearest was pulling around 1,200 visitors a month. This one had 185 in fifteen months. That is not a gap you close with four blog posts.

We weren't losing to better dentists. We were losing to better websites that had a ten year head start.From the project brief, May 2025

We waited for something true to say.

A paid backlink campaign was budgeted and then deliberately shelved. Instead we waited for a real story: the practice's lead dentist being named to a respected regional Top Dentist list for several consecutive years. That one genuine piece of news was syndicated nationally. 960 placements, 7,000 article views and 900 new backlinks, none of them manufactured and none of them bought.

For completeness

What actually got built.

Month 1

Get it counted

GA4, Search Console and Tag Manager built from scratch, with conversion tracking wired straight into the practice's booking widget. 25 technical faults fixed, including insecure URLs and a broken blog structure. Images compressed, alt text added everywhere it was missing. The Google Ads account audited, where calls were found being routed to other practitioners entirely.

Month 2

Rewrite what sells

Keywords mapped against five named local competitors, then 16 pages rebuilt around what patients search. Six core service pages taken past 1,500 words with FAQ schema. New landing pages for implants, veneers, Invisalign and crowns, each titled for the target city. A blog built from nothing and 8 articles published. Meta titles and descriptions rewritten sitewide.

Month 3

Own the map

Google Business Profile rebuilt end to end: hours, services, NAP consistency and a description written with local keywords rather than stuffed with them. A 10 mile service area defined across Montgomery County and the DC border. A genuine review generation workflow, with nothing purchased and nothing incentivised, in line with Google's local quality guidelines.

The counting was the hard part.

Once every call, form and booking click was landing in the practice's own analytics, the argument about whether the marketing works ended. The number is either there or it is not. For fifteen months it has been there, and it is still going.
